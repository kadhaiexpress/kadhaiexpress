South Indian 30 mins 4 servings Vegetarian

Tomato Rasam

Tomato rasam should be thin, tangy, and aromatic — more like a spiced broth than a thick dal. Fresh crush of pepper and cumin makes a clear difference.

Ingredients

  • 3 ripe tomatoes, chopped
  • 1 teaspoon tamarind paste (or small marble of tamarind soaked and squeezed)
  • 1 teaspoon rasam powder, or 1 teaspoon crushed black pepper + 1 teaspoon cumin
  • ½ teaspoon turmeric
  • Salt to taste
  • About 3 cups water
  • 1 dried red chilli
  • 1 teaspoon mustard seeds
  • 1 sprig curry leaves
  • Pinch of hing
  • 1 tablespoon oil or ghee
  • Fresh coriander

Method

  1. In a pot, combine chopped tomatoes, water, turmeric, salt, and tamarind. Bring to a boil and simmer until the tomatoes soften completely, 8–10 minutes. Lightly mash a few pieces to release pulp, but keep the liquid thin.
  2. Add rasam powder (or the crushed pepper-cumin mix). Simmer 4–5 minutes more so the spices bloom into the broth. Do not reduce it into a thick gravy — add hot water if it looks too concentrated.
  3. Taste for salt and tang. The rasam should feel bright and lightly peppery.
  4. In a small pan, heat oil or ghee. Add mustard seeds and wait until they pop. Add dried red chilli, curry leaves, and hing. Pour this tempering over the rasam immediately.
  5. Garnish with coriander. Serve with steamed rice and appalam, or as a light soup in a cup.

Tips for best results

  • Keep rasam thin — it is not sambar.
  • Freshly crushed pepper and cumin taste brighter than old powder alone.
  • A crushed garlic clove in the temper is common in some homes.
