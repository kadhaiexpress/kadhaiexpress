Whole Spices vs Powder: When to Use Which

Indian vegetarian cooking uses both whole spices and powders. Knowing when each shines improves control over aroma and bitterness.

Whole spices

Bay leaf, cinnamon, cloves, cardamom, cumin seeds, and mustard seeds are often fried in oil at the start. They perfume oil and can be left in or fished out. They release flavour more slowly and stay aromatic longer in pulao, biryani, and kadhai dishes.

Powders

Turmeric, red chilli powder, coriander powder, and garam masala blend into sauces. They need enough cooking in oil or gravy to lose raw notes — especially coriander and chilli. Garam masala is often finished at the end so its volatile oils are not driven off.

Practical rule

Start with whole spices in fat for base aroma; use powders for body and colour in the middle; finish with a pinch of garam masala or a tadka for lift. Freshly dry-roasted and ground spices beat stale packets when you have time.

Storage

Whole spices keep longer. Grind small batches. Keep powders airtight, away from heat and light.
