How to Temper Spices (Tadka / Chaunk)

Tempering — tadka or chaunk — is the technique of blooming spices in hot fat and pouring them over dal, chutney, or raita. It is the difference between flat and fragrant Indian vegetarian food.

Why tadka works

Hot oil extracts fat-soluble aromas from cumin, mustard, dried chilli, and curry leaves. Adding this infused oil at the end (or start) layers perfume through the dish without long cooking of delicate herbs.

Basic order

Heat oil or ghee until shimmering but not smoking. Add seeds (mustard, cumin) first — they need heat to crackle. Then dried red chilli, garlic slices, curry leaves, and hing. Powdered spices usually go into the main dish, not the final tadka, unless briefly bloomed.

Common combinations

Dal: ghee, cumin, garlic, red chilli, hing.
South Indian: oil, mustard, urad dal, chana dal, curry leaves, red chilli.
Kadhi: ghee, mustard or cumin, red chilli, curry leaves.

Mistakes to avoid

Burnt garlic turns bitter. Hing burns quickly — add last. Wet curry leaves sputter; stand back. Reheat tadka and pour immediately for maximum aroma.
