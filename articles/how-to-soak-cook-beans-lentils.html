How to Soak and Cook Beans and Lentils

Many vegetarian Indian dishes start with properly cooked legumes. Soaking and cooking time separate creamy rajma from hard centres.

Why soak

Soaking dried beans (rajma, chickpeas) rehydrates them, shortens cook time, and improves even texture. Overnight in plenty of water is standard. Split dals like toor and moong often need only rinsing, though a short soak can help.

Cooking

Pressure cookers are the home standard in India for beans. Cook until a bean mashes easily between fingers. Undercooked kidney beans are hard to digest; cook thoroughly. Salt can be added during cooking for beans that will be simmered again in masala.

Dal texture

For dal tadka, cook until soft enough to whisk lightly. For sambar, dal should be soft but not necessarily fully dissolved. Adjust water for the dish you are making.

Canned vs dried

Canned chickpeas and beans save time. Rinse well; simmer longer in the masala so they pick up flavour. Dried legumes remain cheaper and often tastier when cooked well.
