Aloo Paratha

Aloo paratha fails when the dough is dry or the filling is wet. Soft dough, cool dry filling, and steady tawa heat give even browning and a sealed edge.

Ingredients

  • 2 cups whole wheat atta
  • Water to knead
  • 1 tsp oil (optional, in dough)
  • 3 medium potatoes, boiled and peeled
  • 1 tsp cumin powder
  • ½ tsp red chilli powder
  • ½ tsp amchur
  • ½ tsp garam masala
  • Salt
  • Chopped coriander
  • Oil or ghee for cooking

Method

  1. Knead atta with water (and optional oil) into a soft, smooth dough. Cover and rest 15–20 minutes so it rolls without springing back.
  2. Mash potatoes while still warm, then let steam escape so the filling is dry. Mix in cumin powder, chilli, amchur, garam masala, salt, and coriander. Taste the filling — it should be slightly bold because the dough is plain.
  3. Divide dough into equal balls. Roll one ball into a small disc, place a spoon of filling in the centre, gather edges, and seal tightly. Flatten gently, dust with flour, and roll from the centre outward to an even circle. If filling leaks, dust and continue carefully.
  4. Heat a tawa on medium. Place the paratha; cook until light bubbles appear. Flip, apply oil or ghee, and cook until golden spots form on both sides. Press gently with a spatula for even colour.
  5. Stack cooked parathas in a covered box or cloth. Serve with butter, curd, and pickle.

Tips for best results

  • Cool, dry filling prevents tears.
  • Soft dough is easier to seal than stiff dough.
  • Medium heat browns without burning raw centres.
