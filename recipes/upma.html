Rava Upma

Upma should be soft and fluffy, not pasty. Dry-roasting the rava and adding it slowly to boiling seasoned water are the two steps that control texture.

Ingredients

  • 1 cup rava (sooji / semolina)
  • 2½ to 3 cups water (start with 2½ for firmer upma)
  • 1 medium onion, finely chopped
  • Optional vegetables: carrot, peas, beans (½ cup total)
  • 1 teaspoon mustard seeds
  • 1 teaspoon urad dal
  • 1–2 green chillies, chopped
  • 1 teaspoon grated ginger
  • 1 sprig curry leaves
  • 2 tablespoons oil
  • Salt to taste
  • Lemon juice and coriander, to finish

Method

  1. In a dry pan on medium-low heat, roast the rava until it smells nutty and feels light, 4–6 minutes. Do not let it brown deeply. Transfer to a plate and set aside. Roasting removes the raw taste and reduces gumminess.
  2. In the same pan, heat oil. Add mustard seeds; when they pop, add urad dal and fry until light golden. Add green chilli, ginger, and curry leaves. Sauté the onion until soft. Add optional vegetables and cook 2–3 minutes.
  3. Pour in the measured water and salt. Bring to a rolling boil.
  4. Lower the heat. With one hand, rain the roasted rava into the water in a steady stream while stirring constantly with the other hand to prevent lumps. Cover and cook on low heat for 2–3 minutes until the water is absorbed and the upma looks soft.
  5. Fluff with a spoon. Add lemon juice and coriander. Rest covered 2 minutes, then serve with coconut chutney or a banana on the side.

Tips for best results

  • Always roast rava before cooking.
  • Water ratio controls softness — add a splash of hot water if it sets too firm.
  • Stirring while adding rava is what prevents lumps.
