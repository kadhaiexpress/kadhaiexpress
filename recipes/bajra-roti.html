Bajra Roti (Pearl Millet Flatbread)

Bajra roti is a rustic millet flatbread. Softness comes from warm water, small dough portions, and gentle patting rather than aggressive rolling.

Ingredients

  • 2 cups bajra (pearl millet) flour
  • Warm water as needed
  • Salt to taste
  • Ghee, to finish

Method

  1. Keep the bajra flour in a bowl. If the flour feels cold and dry, warm it slightly by stirring in a dry pan for a minute on low heat, then return to the bowl. This helps the dough bind.
  2. Add salt. Pour warm water a little at a time and knead into a soft, pliable dough. Millet dough dries and cracks quickly, so knead only as much as you will roll in the next few minutes. Cover the rest.
  3. Divide into small balls. On a banana leaf, plastic sheet, or floured board, pat each ball with wet or lightly oiled hands into a medium-thick round. If it cracks at the edges, pinch and smooth with damp fingers.
  4. Heat a tawa on medium heat. Place the roti carefully. Cook until the colour changes and small bubbles appear. Flip and cook the other side. Optional: finish briefly on direct flame for a light char, or press gently on the tawa.
  5. Smear with ghee while hot. Serve with dal, saag, garlic chutney, or jaggery.

Tips for best results

  • Work in small batches — bajra dough hardens if left open.
  • Warm water binds better than cold water.
  • Do not expect the stretch of wheat; patting is traditional and kinder than thin rolling.
