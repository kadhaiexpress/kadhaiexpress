Methi Thepla

Methi thepla should stay soft for hours. Oil in the dough, finely chopped methi, and gentle cooking on the tawa are more important than extra spices.

Ingredients

  • 2 cups whole wheat flour (atta)
  • 1 cup fresh methi (fenugreek) leaves, washed and finely chopped
  • ½ teaspoon turmeric powder
  • 1 teaspoon red chilli powder (adjust to taste)
  • 1 teaspoon coriander-cumin powder
  • 1 tablespoon oil (plus more for cooking)
  • Salt to taste
  • Yogurt or water, as needed to knead
  • Optional: 1 teaspoon sesame seeds, grated ginger

Method

  1. In a wide bowl, mix atta, chopped methi, turmeric, chilli powder, coriander-cumin powder, salt, optional sesame and ginger, and 1 tablespoon oil. Rub the oil into the flour so it looks slightly crumbly — this helps softness later.
  2. Add yogurt or water a little at a time and knead into a soft, pliable dough. It should be softer than stiff chapati dough but not sticky. Cover and rest 15–20 minutes.
  3. Divide into equal balls. On a lightly floured surface, roll each ball into a thin even circle. Dust lightly only as needed; too much dry flour makes dry theplas.
  4. Heat a tawa on medium heat. Place a thepla on it. When small bubbles appear, flip. Apply a little oil on the cooked side, flip again, and press gently with a spatula until light brown spots appear on both sides. Do not cook on very high heat or the outside browns before the inside softens.
  5. Stack theplas in a covered box or wrap in a clean cloth so steam keeps them soft. Serve with yogurt, pickle, or tea. They travel well once fully cooled.

Tips for best results

  • Finely chopped methi distributes better than large stems.
  • Oil in the dough is the main reason theplas stay soft.
  • Kasuri methi (dried) can substitute in a pinch; use less, as it is stronger.
