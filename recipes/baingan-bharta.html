North Indian 50 mins 3–4 servings Vegetarian

Baingan Bharta (Smoky Mashed Eggplant)

Baingan bharta gets its character from smoky roasted eggplant mashed into a simple onion-tomato masala. Roasting properly and cooking off excess water are what separate a watery bharta from a thick, fragrant one.

Ingredients

  • 1 large eggplant (baingan), about 400–500 g
  • 2 medium onions, finely chopped
  • 2 medium tomatoes, finely chopped
  • 1 tablespoon ginger-garlic paste
  • 2 green chillies, slit or chopped
  • 1 teaspoon cumin seeds
  • ½ teaspoon turmeric powder
  • 1 teaspoon coriander powder
  • ½ teaspoon red chilli powder (optional)
  • 2 tablespoons oil
  • Salt to taste
  • Fresh coriander leaves, chopped
  • Lemon wedge, to serve

Method

  1. Wash and dry the eggplant. Using a knife tip, make 3–4 small slits so steam can escape. If you have a gas flame, place the eggplant directly on a medium flame and turn with tongs every few minutes until the skin is fully charred and the flesh collapses when pressed (15–20 minutes). For an oven or air-fryer, roast at high heat until the skin blisters and the centre is soft. The goal is soft flesh and a light smoky aroma.
  2. Cool the eggplant until you can handle it. Peel away all the blackened skin. Discard the stem. Mash the flesh with a fork or knife on a board — keep a slightly chunky texture, not a baby-food puree. Check for and remove any hard seed clusters if they bother you.
  3. Heat oil in a kadhai on medium heat. Add cumin seeds and let them crackle. Add the chopped onions and cook, stirring often, until they turn light golden. Add ginger-garlic paste and green chilli; cook for about a minute until the raw smell softens.
  4. Add the tomatoes, turmeric, coriander powder, optional chilli powder, and salt. Cook until the tomatoes break down completely and the mixture looks thick, with oil starting to show at the edges. This base should not taste raw.
  5. Add the mashed eggplant. Mix well so every bit is coated with masala. Cook on medium heat for 8–12 minutes, stirring often. If the mixture looks watery, keep cooking uncovered until it thickens and the oil lightly separates. Taste and adjust salt.
  6. Finish with chopped coriander and a squeeze of lemon if you like brightness. Serve hot with roti, paratha, or dal-rice.

Tips for best results

  • Open-flame roasting gives the best smoky flavour; oven roasting is milder but still good.
  • Always cook off extra moisture after adding the mash — watery bharta tastes flat.
  • A pinch of garam masala at the end is optional, not required.
