Rajma Masala (Kidney Bean Curry)

Rajma masala is soft red kidney beans in a deep onion-tomato gravy. The difference between average and excellent rajma is fully cooked beans and onions browned patiently until sweet and golden.

Ingredients

  • 1½ cups dried red kidney beans, soaked 8–12 hours (or 2 cans, rinsed)
  • Water for cooking
  • 2 large onions, finely chopped
  • 2 ripe tomatoes, pureed (or very finely chopped)
  • 1 tablespoon ginger-garlic paste
  • 2 tablespoons oil or ghee
  • 1 teaspoon cumin seeds
  • 1 teaspoon coriander powder
  • ½ teaspoon turmeric
  • 1 teaspoon red chilli powder (adjust to taste)
  • 1 teaspoon garam masala
  • 1 teaspoon kitchen king or rajma masala (optional)
  • Salt to taste
  • Fresh coriander and ginger juliennes, to finish

Method

  1. Soak dried rajma in plenty of water for at least 8 hours or overnight. Drain and rinse. If using canned beans, rinse well and skip to the masala steps, but simmer longer later so they absorb flavour.
  2. Pressure-cook soaked rajma with fresh water and a little salt until very soft — a bean should mash easily when pressed. This usually takes several whistles. Reserve the cooking liquid; it is full of starch and flavour for the gravy.
  3. Heat oil or ghee in a heavy kadhai on medium heat. Add cumin seeds. When they crackle, add the chopped onions. Cook slowly, stirring often, until the onions turn a deep golden brown. This can take 10–15 minutes and is the backbone of the gravy colour and taste.
  4. Add ginger-garlic paste and cook for a minute until the raw smell goes. Pour in the tomato puree. Cook, stirring, until the mixture thickens and you see oil separating from the masala at the sides of the pan.
  5. Add turmeric, coriander powder, red chilli powder, optional rajma masala, and salt. Stir for 30–40 seconds so the powdered spices fry gently in the oil without burning.
  6. Add the cooked rajma along with enough reserved cooking liquid to make a thick, spoon-coating gravy. Mash 8–10 beans against the side of the pan to thicken the gravy naturally.
  7. Simmer uncovered on low heat for 15–20 minutes, stirring occasionally. The beans should look coated and the gravy rich. Finish with garam masala and chopped coriander. Rest 5 minutes before serving with steamed rice, onion slices, and lemon.

Tips for best results

  • Undercooked rajma is hard and hard to digest — always cook until fully soft.
  • Deep golden onions matter more than extra powdered spices.
  • Rajma tastes better after resting; reheat gently with a splash of water if needed.
