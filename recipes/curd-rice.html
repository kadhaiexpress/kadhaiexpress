Curd Rice (Thayir Sadam)

Curd rice should be cool, creamy, and gently tempered. Slightly soft rice, fresh yogurt, and a milk splash keep it from turning chalky or overly sour.

Ingredients

  • 2 cups cooked rice (preferably a little soft)
  • 1 to 1½ cups fresh thick yogurt
  • 2–4 tablespoons milk (as needed)
  • Salt to taste
  • For tempering: 1 teaspoon oil, 1 teaspoon mustard seeds, 1 teaspoon urad dal, 1 teaspoon grated ginger, 1 green chilli chopped, 1 sprig curry leaves, pinch of hing
  • Optional garnish: pomegranate arils, chopped coriander, grated carrot

Method

  1. If the rice is freshly cooked, mash it lightly while still warm so some grains break — this helps the yogurt cling. Let it cool until just lukewarm; mixing yogurt into piping-hot rice can make it watery and dull the flavour.
  2. Whisk the yogurt with salt until smooth. Stir in a little milk if the yogurt is very thick. Mix the yogurt into the rice until creamy. Adjust with more yogurt or milk until the consistency is soft and spoonable, not stiff.
  3. Heat oil in a small pan. Add mustard seeds and wait until they pop. Add urad dal and fry until golden. Add ginger, green chilli, curry leaves, and hing. Sauté a few seconds until fragrant — do not burn the ginger.
  4. Pour the tempering over the curd rice and fold in gently. Taste for salt.
  5. Garnish as you like. Serve at room temperature with pickle or fried chillies. For later, refrigerate and loosen with a spoon of milk before serving if it tightens up.

Tips for best results

  • Fresh yogurt tastes better than very sour curd for this dish.
  • Tempering is the flavour — ginger and curry leaves matter.
  • Slightly overcooked rice mixes more smoothly than firm separate grains.
