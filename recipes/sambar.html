Sambar (South Indian Lentil Vegetable Stew)

Sambar is a tangy dal-and-vegetable stew at the centre of many South Indian meals. Balance matters: soft dal, bright tamarind, enough sambar powder, and a hot tempering of mustard and curry leaves.

Ingredients

  • ¾ cup toor dal, rinsed
  • ½ teaspoon turmeric
  • 1 lemon-sized ball of tamarind, soaked in warm water (or 1–2 teaspoons paste)
  • 2 cups mixed vegetables (drumstick, pumpkin, carrot, brinjal, beans — use what you have)
  • 2–3 tablespoons sambar powder (good quality)
  • Salt to taste
  • Small pinch of jaggery (optional, for balance)
  • 1 teaspoon mustard seeds
  • 2 dried red chillies
  • 1 sprig curry leaves
  • Pinch of hing
  • 1 tablespoon oil
  • Optional: 1 small onion or a few shallots, 1 tomato

Method

  1. Cook toor dal with turmeric and enough water until completely soft. Mash lightly with the back of a ladle so the dal thickens the sambar but still has some body.
  2. While the dal cooks, soak tamarind in warm water for 15 minutes and squeeze out thick tamarind water; discard fibres. Alternatively dissolve tamarind paste in water.
  3. In another pot, cook the vegetables in water with a little salt until just tender — they should hold shape, not turn to mush. Drumstick and pumpkin are classic; carrot and beans work well too.
  4. Add the cooked dal and tamarind water to the vegetables. Stir in sambar powder. Bring to a gentle boil and simmer 8–10 minutes so the powder cooks through and the flavours marry. Add water if the sambar is too thick, or simmer longer if too thin. Taste for salt and tang; add a pinch of jaggery if the tamarind feels sharp.
  5. For the tempering, heat oil in a small pan. Add mustard seeds and wait until they pop. Add dried red chillies, curry leaves, and hing. Pour this immediately over the sambar.
  6. Rest a few minutes before serving. Ladle over idli, dosa, or hot rice with a little ghee if you like.

Tips for best results

  • Fresh or good store-bought sambar powder makes a large difference.
  • Do not skip the final tempering — it carries the aroma.
  • Vegetable mix can change with season; keep dal and tamarind balance steady.
