Dal Tadka (Restaurant-Style Yellow Dal)

Dal tadka is everyday comfort: soft yellow lentils finished with a hot tempering of ghee, cumin, garlic, and red chilli. This version walks through cooking the dal properly, building a simple masala, and pouring a fragrant tadka at the end so the aroma hits the table.

Ingredients

  • 1 cup toor dal (arhar / split pigeon peas), rinsed until water runs clearer
  • 3 to 3½ cups water, plus more as needed
  • ½ teaspoon turmeric powder
  • 1 teaspoon salt, or to taste
  • 1 tablespoon ghee or oil (for the masala base)
  • 1 teaspoon cumin seeds
  • 1 medium onion, finely chopped
  • 1 medium tomato, finely chopped
  • 1–2 green chillies, slit
  • 1 teaspoon ginger-garlic paste (or finely minced)
  • 1 teaspoon coriander powder
  • ½ teaspoon red chilli powder (optional)
  • For final tadka: 1 tablespoon ghee, 1 teaspoon cumin seeds, 4–5 garlic cloves thinly sliced, 1–2 dried red chillies, pinch of hing (asafoetida), optional
  • Fresh coriander leaves, chopped
  • Lemon wedges, to serve

Method

  1. Rinse the toor dal in several changes of water until the water is less cloudy. This removes excess surface starch and helps the dal cook cleanly.
  2. Add the dal to a pressure cooker or heavy pot with 3 cups water, turmeric, and salt. Pressure-cook for 3–4 whistles until the grains are soft enough to mash easily between your fingers. On the stovetop without a cooker, simmer gently for 35–45 minutes, skimming any foam that rises.
  3. Once cooked, whisk the dal lightly with a spoon or whisk so it becomes creamy but not completely pasty. If it looks too thick, stir in hot water a little at a time until it is pourable but still rich. Keep it on a low simmer.
  4. In a separate kadhai, heat 1 tablespoon ghee or oil on medium heat. Add cumin seeds and let them crackle. Add onion and cook, stirring often, until the onion turns light golden — do not rush this step; pale onion leaves the dal tasting raw.
  5. Add ginger-garlic paste and green chilli. Stir for 30–40 seconds until the raw smell softens. Add tomato and cook until it breaks down and the mixture looks thick, with oil beginning to separate at the edges.
  6. Sprinkle coriander powder and optional red chilli powder. Stir for 20–30 seconds so the spices bloom in the oil. Pour this masala into the simmering dal and mix well. Let the dal bubble gently for 5–8 minutes so the flavours settle. Taste and adjust salt.
  7. For the finishing tadka, heat 1 tablespoon ghee in a small pan. Add cumin seeds. When they crackle, add sliced garlic and dried red chillies. Fry on medium-low until the garlic turns golden (not dark brown). Switch off the heat and add hing if using.
  8. Immediately pour the hot tadka over the dal. You should hear a brief sizzle. Do not stir it away completely if you like a streak of aroma on top. Garnish with coriander and serve with rice or roti and a squeeze of lemon.

Tips for best results

  • The final garlic-cumin tadka is what makes home dal taste restaurant-like — use ghee if you can.
  • If the dal thickens after sitting, loosen with hot water and a pinch of salt before serving.
  • For a vegan version, use oil instead of ghee throughout.
  • Slightly overcooked dal is better than undercooked; hard centres ruin the texture.
